What is it

What an Avalanche bridge does

An Avalanche bridge moves value between Ethereum and the Avalanche C-Chain, the EVM chain where most Avalanche DeFi and wallets live. The two networks do not share state, so a token cannot simply travel; a bridge protocol locks or burns it on one side and releases or mints a matching token on the other.

What this interface does

This page quotes routes across bridge protocols, shows the expected amount received, and sends the transactions to your wallet. It lets you pick a token such as ETH, USDC, USDT or BTC.b, a direction (Ethereum → Avalanche or back), and an amount.

Who it is for

Anyone who needs funds on Avalanche C-Chain to use a dApp, or who wants to bring them back to Ethereum. You will also need a little AVAX on the Avalanche side to pay for gas.

Native vs bridged

Native vs bridged tokens on Avalanche

The token you receive depends on the route. Some assets are issued natively on Avalanche, while others arrive as bridged versions, often marked with a suffix such as .e or .b. They can look alike in a wallet but are different contracts.

Why it matters

A dApp or pool may accept only one version of a token. If you hold the other, you may need to swap between them on a DEX before you can use it, so check which version your destination expects before you bridge.

How to bridge

How to bridge to Avalanche

Route and quote

The interface compares available bridge routes for your token and direction, then shows the amount you should receive, the fees included in the quote, and the estimated time. Nothing is signed until you confirm.

Step by step

  1. Connect your wallet and choose the source chain, the destination chain and the token.
  2. Enter an amount and review the route, the received token and the fees in the quote.
  3. Approve the token once if the route needs an ERC-20 allowance from this wallet.
  4. Sign the bridge transaction on the source chain and wait for it to confirm.
  5. Wait for the funds to arrive on the destination chain, then check the received token in your wallet.

Bridging to Avalanche takes you to C-Chain, so add the token to your wallet if it does not appear automatically.

Fees & gas

What bridging costs

Gas on both chains

The first transaction is paid in ETH when you start from Ethereum. Any later action on Avalanche C-Chain, including swaps or approvals, is paid in AVAX. See how gas works on Ethereum.

Bridge and protocol fees

Each route can include a bridge or relayer fee, a protocol fee, or a spread in the exchange rate. These are reflected in the quoted amount received, so compare that figure rather than the amount you send.

Get AVAX before you need it

Holding bridged USDC or WETH.e does not pay for gas on C-Chain. If your wallet has no AVAX, you will not be able to approve or swap after you arrive, so plan a small AVAX amount first.

Security

Staying safe when you bridge

Non-custodial by design

You connect your own wallet and sign every transaction yourself. This interface routes and quotes; it never holds your funds or your keys.

Before you sign

  • Check the destination chain and the token you will receive, since .e, .b and native tokens are different contracts.
  • Verify token contract addresses; lookalike tokens are common.
  • Approve only what you need, and revoke old ERC-20 allowances you no longer use.
  • Try a small test amount first on a route you have not used before.
  • Bridges rely on smart contracts and validators, so risk is never zero.
FAQ

Avalanche Bridge FAQ

What is the Avalanche Bridge?

An Avalanche bridge moves tokens between Ethereum and the Avalanche C-Chain by locking or burning them on one chain and releasing or minting them on the other. This page finds a route through bridge protocols and sends the transactions to your wallet to sign.

Which tokens can I bridge between Ethereum and Avalanche?

Common choices include ETH (received as WETH.e), USDC, USDT and BTC.b, plus other tokens that a supported route covers. Availability depends on the route and the direction, so the quote shows what can be bridged.

What do .e and .b mean on Avalanche tokens?

They mark tokens that were bridged to Avalanche, such as WETH.e or BTC.b, rather than issued natively on it. They are separate contracts from native versions of the same asset, so check which one a dApp expects.

Which token do I need to pay gas on Avalanche C-Chain?

Gas on Avalanche C-Chain is paid in AVAX. Holding a bridged token like USDC or WETH.e does not cover it, so keep a small AVAX balance in the same wallet.

How long does bridging take?

It depends on the route, the direction and network conditions, and the estimate in the quote is only a guide. You need to wait for confirmation on the source chain and then for the funds to appear on the destination chain.

Does this site hold my funds while I bridge?

No. The interface is non-custodial: you connect your own wallet and sign each transaction. Your tokens are handled by the bridge protocol contracts you approve, not by this page.
